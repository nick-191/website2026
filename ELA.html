my food

The dish combines many different ingredients. In my family, we make it with chips, green salsa,
queso fresco, Mexican rice, avocado, eggs, carne asada, and sour cream. It's been in my dad's side
of the family for a while, given to my grandmother, then my dad and his sisters, then to me.
It's been passed down from generation to generation, and I see it as not only a meal but a way
for us to connect. When it's about school, work drama, or just issues, it never fails us to bring
us together. Entering the home of my grandmother, standing in the kitchen making food. As I set my
bag down, the smell of eggs, chips, carne asada, and rice filled my nose and made an amazing
combination. I sat down and saw her making chilaquiles, one of my favorite dishes. She called me
over to the table and had me sit down with a plate. Topped with crumbly queso fresco, sour cream, and
her homemade salsa. I take a bite and feel the crunchy but soft tortilla chips, the tender asada, the warm
egg yolk, the creamy sour cream, the salsa- spicy how I like it- the rice cooked to perfection, and the whole
meal itself bringing me back to when I was a child trying it for the first time. I made sure to enjoy every
bite. When I finished, I sat down on the couch with little bits of rice and queso fresco on my shirt. I
thanked my grandma for the meal while the aroma still lingered in the air, wondering to myself if I should
get more. But I tell myself that a meal like this should be cherished and not abused to eat everyday as
I wouldn't wanna get over, like I will get over it anytime soon, I tell myself. As I'm sitting on the
couch, I begin to feel drowsy and tired; my eyes feel heavy, and I drift to sleep. The first record of
Chilaquiles was in a book published in 1831 because it showed many different ways to make them, such as
Chilaquiles blancos, rojos/colroajos, reirnos, and tapatios. There is also a piece of evidence that the
chile sauces were already made in 1571 during the colonial period. The word Chilaquiles comes from a mix of
chili, which is water, and quilitl, which is an edible plant. I got my sources from Loarouse cocina,
Werriam Webstar, UNAM, Gran Diccioniro Nahuatl-Qulitl. Chilaquiles are a very popular Mexican/Spanish
breakfast dish known not just for its taste but for how it brings families together and how it is passed
down through many generations. As time passed, so did people. Many Mexican and Spanish people started
migrating to other countries, bringing their cultures with them during the 20th century. Restaurants,
markets, family, and local businesses started getting more attention, and it started to spread like
wildfire. Chilaquiles don't have a right way to make them since many families make them in their own
way, and usually my family likes to cook them daily. It was a warm morning. I was with my grandmother,
waiting for her to finish cooking the meal. She called me over, and as little me ran to the kitchen, I
could smell the aroma of food filling the kitchen. It was new and strange, but it smelled amazing. I sat
down, and in front of me was something new, something called chilaquiles. I took a bite and immediately fell
in love as it melted in my mouth. It tasted amazing, something I've never had, and I wanted more. Fast forward
to today, I've never given up the love, and so has my family. Every so often, my grandma will cook it for my
family and me. When it's for just breakfast, a holiday, or going camping for the weekend. But it never fails
to do one thing. Bring us together when we're laughing over jokes, talking about work problems, chisme(gossip in Spanish),
or just talking about life. It's always a good morning when we have it. But it never stops reminding me of my own childhood; if
only I could go back and have it for the first time again, if only, after so many years of trying this dish, and first trying it for
the first time. I'll never get over the special dish that's brought me so much joy in my life. Every time I eat it brings me back to when
I was a kid. I've had many different chilaquiles, but none of them will ever compare to when I first had them from my grandma. None of them
taste the same as hers, and I'm glad I was able to learn more about my favorite, and I hope to learn more about it in the future as I get older.
